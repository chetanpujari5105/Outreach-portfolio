# Chetan’s business website portfolio
## Build and outreach plan · 23 November 2026

Build one portfolio with a different front door for each business. A café owner opens a café menu on their phone. A salon owner tries choosing a service and time. A shop owner who asks about billing builds a sample invoice. Each can see what Chetan built, try the part that matters, and ask for a version for their business.

**First release to ship** — one salon page with a working booking demo, the actual Nukkad Naai project, and a working route back to Chetan. That single link can serve the next approved salon batch. Follow with the café menu and the Manakar practice case. The rest of the catalog can grow while those links are in use. All routes below are proposed paths.

## 2. Decide what each business should open

Choose the link using the business type **and** the job the owner cares about. Site ownership changes the offer. It need not disqualify a business from the portfolio.

| What you know | What to show | Offer to make |
|---|---|---|
| No website found after checking Maps and the business profile | An industry example with services or products, location and one customer action | A free sample homepage with that action for their business |
| Website exists and a specific improvement is visible | The relevant task on a familiar type of site | A sample of that one addition, such as a readable menu or trial-class enquiry |
| Website and bookings already work | If the owner mentions another need, open its example, such as billing, corporate orders or staff enquiries | A small demonstration of the distinct need. Apply the existing booking-outreach skip rule to the current campaign. |
| Website status unknown | A relevant example with neutral wording | Ask whether that workflow would help. Avoid guessing what is missing. |
| New industry or unclear fit | A working example for the same job, with its actual industry visible, or the work index | Ask which task takes their team the most time. Record the missing example for the next build cycle. |

For example, a bakery that takes celebration-cake orders needs a date, serving count and reference brief. A walk-in coffee shop may want opening hours and a readable menu. Both fit the food category. Sending the same table-booking pitch to both wastes the advantage of having an industry page.

**Mobile page recipe.** Show a short industry headline, one real screen at a legible size, a proof label, and a specific button together. Immediately beneath them, explain the customer task and offer “Make a free sample for my business.” Below that place related work, one short paragraph about Chetan, and contact. Use three primary navigation links — Work, Business types and Request a sample. Business types can point to the industry section of the index at launch.

**Visual direction.** Keep the portfolio shell quiet and consistent. Let the examples look like the businesses they serve. The salon demo can open on a service list with duration and price. The café can open on an editorial menu with food photography, hours and location. The billing demo should open on a readable invoice with editable line items. Capture screens from the working builds. Use owned or licensed photography for the fictional brands. On a small phone, a visitor should be able to read the menu item or appointment time directly in the image.

**Case-page writing pattern.** One sentence on the business, one on the customer’s task, two or three captioned screens, a sentence on Chetan’s contribution and one precise delivery statement. For Manakar, a defensible caption is “A visitor can choose a service and preferred time while requesting an appointment.” Add before-and-after figures only when there is evidence linking the change to the result.

## 7. Turn a “yes” into a scoped paid project

1. **Receive the request.** For an Instagram lead, invite them to reply “yes” in the same conversation. On the site, offer a short form for business name, one reply method and the task they want. Make an existing website optional. Show a receipt only after the request is saved. Preserve input if delivery fails.
2. **Agree on one task.** Chetan confirms the intended customer action, the current process and the material the owner wants used. For a café, ask “For the sample, I’ll show your drinks menu and a group-table enquiry. Which menu should I use?” Record a delivery date that fits actual capacity.
3. **Make the free sample.** Adapt one screen and one short interaction using the agreed material. Keep prices and times marked as illustrative unless supplied by the owner. Give the preview an unlisted link and disable indexing. Use access control for anything confidential.
4. **Review on the owner’s phone.** Deliver the link in the same conversation. Ask one concrete question, such as “Would you want these enquiries to reach WhatsApp or your front desk?” Record requested changes and interest.
5. **Quote the production version.** List pages, working integrations, domain/hosting responsibility, delivery milestones, revision allowance, support, currency and one-time versus recurring charges. A salon booking system needs real availability, staff control, cancellation rules and notifications. Price those explicitly after the sample review.
6. **Hand over and grow the library.** Test the agreed tasks with the owner, hand over account access and operating instructions, and request permission to show the finished work. Update the case page with actual screenshots and any evidenced outcome.

Extend the existing outreach records with a sales stage for each opportunity. Move it from interested through sample agreed, sample delivered and proposal sent, then mark it won or closed. Chetan owns replies and commercial decisions. The builder can prepare previews, proof assets and draft scope documents.

## 8. Make the same portfolio usable worldwide

- Launch in plain English. The main site can say “Based in Pune, available for remote projects.” Keep local proof local. The Hadapsar practice is valuable precisely because it is specific.
- Store copy, locale, currency, business time zone and contact methods as separate fields. Show the business time zone when a visitor chooses an appointment. Display the invoice currency on the invoice itself.
- Test one India fixture and one international fixture, for example a Pune salon using INR and a UK studio using GBP. Change the date display, phone format, opening hours and contact preference together. A demo brand or locale never counts as a customer reference.
- Let an owner choose email, a relevant messaging channel or a call. Use email as an accessible fallback for owners who do not use WhatsApp or Instagram. Offer translations when a specific outreach batch needs them and a fluent reviewer can check the offer and forms.
- Keep country-by-country payment handling, taxes, invoice rules and production data collection in the individual project scope. The public portfolio can demonstrate the workflow with fictional records before those implementation choices are made.

## 11. Launch checks and feedback loop

- Open the exact DM link on a phone from Instagram’s in-app browser. Check its share preview, first screen and return path. Run the same task in mobile Safari or Chrome and on desktop.
- At 360 px width and at 200% zoom, read the service name, price, controls and outcome. Complete the task using the keyboard. Check focus, labels, errors, loading, reduced motion and screen-reader confirmation.
- Test direct refresh, back, repeat taps, invalid input, demo reset, slow network, one missing image and one failed request. Check that a visitor can still reach Chetan. A useful launch target is a readable headline and primary action within 2.5 seconds in a documented mobile performance test.
- Ask a business owner or representative to open a link without coaching. Can they tell who it is for, whether the work is real or illustrative, complete the featured task, and find the free sample offer? Record where they pause and fix the biggest obstacle before scaling that page.
- Group confirmed first DMs, positive replies, samples agreed, samples delivered, proposals and paid projects by industry and week. Record aggregate page visits and demo completions separately. Keep Instagram handles, names and contact details out of public URLs and analytics events.
- Review **positive replies ÷ confirmed first DMs**, **samples delivered ÷ samples agreed**, and **paid projects ÷ proposals** using cohorts old enough to have progressed. Show numerator and denominator. A small daily batch is a learning signal. Avoid drawing a conversion conclusion from one day.
- Use the drop-off to choose the next task. Few replies after several comparable batches → review relevance of the lead and first example. Owners ask for samples but delivery lags → simplify the free scope. Owners like samples but rarely accept quotes → review production scope and handoff with them. A repeated request for a specific workflow moves that demo up the queue.

**First build ticket.** Create the draft salon project record, select one Nukkad Naai screen that shows the radio experience, and sketch the service-to-time selector using fictional salon data. Review those three items together on a phone. Once the real project and the proposed booking feature are clear at a glance, implement that route end to end.
